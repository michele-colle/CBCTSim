The phantom set

Material labels

Labels 0–4 are assigned by thresholding the Hounsfield volume; labels 5, 10 and 11 are geometric bodies written after classification and are therefore independent of the thresholds and of any noise treatment.

Tissue densities for the mesh phantoms are the phantom’s own age-specific values; cortical bone spans roughly 1.5 g/cm³ in the newborn to 1.90 g/cm³ in the adult. Patient-derived phantoms use the adult female reference set throughout.

Hounsfield values are discrete, by construction A mesh phantom assigns one attenuation coefficient per tissue, so its Hounsfield volume is a set of discrete values rather than a continuous distribution. At 60 keV monoenergetic, cortical bone reaches 1500–4700 HU against 1000–2000 HU in clinical polychromatic CT, with an empty interval between spongiosa and cortical bone. Label assignment is unaffected, but mesh- and patient-derived phantoms are not comparable at the Hounsfield level.

Reconstructed field of view

By similar triangles the reconstructed FOV at isocenter is only ±85.9 mm tall, against a 250 mm output volume. Anatomy placed against the top face of the volume rather than against the beam therefore left approximately 64 mm of head outside the reconstructed field and invisible to every projection. 

FOV half-height = (detector height / 2) × (SAD / SDD)
                = 146.7 mm            × (578.0 / 987.0)  =  85.909 mm

The placement rule adopted for the whole set anchors the detected cranial vertex at 75.909 mm above isocenter — 10 mm of air below the top edge of the reconstructed field — and centres the transverse position on the bounding box of the uppermost 100 mm of anatomy, so the head is centred as in a real head examination rather than being pulled off-axis by the shoulders.

Patient support

The support cross-section was reconstructed from real CBCT projection data rather than from a drawing: a rounded trapezoid 440 mm wide at the top and 396 mm at the base, 57 mm high, formed from a 1.5 mm carbon wall around a polyurethane foam core, with a 4 mm chamfer and 5 mm corner radii.

It is seated automatically behind the measured posterior surface of the head with a 2 mm clearance. Across the 66-case patient batch the resulting support position ranged from 119.3 to 150.6 mm posterior to isocenter, tracking head depth. Sampled displacements around that seat generate the support-position variants, each verified to clear all tissue voxels by at least the nominal 2 mm before being written.

Anatomical sources

ICRP mesh series

Twelve tetrahedral reference phantoms covering newborn to adult in both sexes. Attenuation is computed per tissue at 60 keV and converted to Hounsfield units; the adult female phantom alone resolves 227 distinct tissue materials before they are pooled into the five-tissue scheme. The captured anatomy spans a fixed 320.1 mm cranio-caudal window from the vertex downward, so the transverse extents below measure body size across the series.

Patient computed tomography

Eighty-six head examinations from two archives: ten from GradientHealth and seventy-six from CQ500. Slice pitch is 0.625 mm nominal; twelve further CQ500 candidates were rejected for a Z sampling of 3 mm or coarser, an order of magnitude above the 0.3 mm output voxel. Two of the ten full-cranium CQ500 cases are recomposed from two partial acquisitions of the same series, so that group is eight native series plus two rebuilt volumes.

Every case carries a tissue mask removing the couch and head cradle, which would otherwise enter the phantom as tissue. Provenance differs by archive: the ten GradientHealth cases were segmented by hand in 3D Slicer; the CQ500 cases use threshold-and-largest-component masks from the source curation, of which 22 in the partial-cranium group were re-segmented because they had included the cradle or the rail.

Gantry tilt is accounted for. A quarter of the CQ500 partial-cranium series were acquired with a tilted gantry, between 4° and 24°. The acquisition’s direction cosines are resolved during resampling, so tilted series are reproduced without shear — tissue extents match the geometry declared in the series headers to better than half a millimetre on every case.

Field truncation is inherent to clinical data. Acquired to answer a clinical question rather than for dosimetry, a proportion of these series are truncated, most often at the vertex. Every case is inspected before it enters the set; those retained have truncation that is minimal and does not affect the simulated projections.

Quality control

Placement errors do not raise exceptions: a coordinate-frame inconsistency can put the anatomy entirely outside the beam while every reported offset remains plausible. Each volume therefore carries a rendered sagittal section with the beam overlaid, and each batch is additionally screened on distribution statistics.

A vertex anchor identical to four decimal places across every case confirms that field-of-view anchoring engaged in all of them; a tissue fraction near zero would indicate anatomy placed outside the volume. Sections were then reviewed individually for the flagged cases and for the numerical extremes. 327 sections have been rendered and reviewed across the set.

Noise treatment

Hard thresholds applied to clinical CT convert quantum noise into isolated misclassified voxels, each of which becomes a spurious material boundary for the transport calculation. A 3×3×3 median filter followed by removal of connected components smaller than 20 voxels — applied to air, spongiosa and cortical bone only, and always preserving the largest component of each — removes 99.5% of sub-20-voxel speckle and reclassifies 3.2% of voxels. Both stages act on the Hounsfield volume before resampling, and before the anatomy is located, so vertex detection is not misled by noise.

A volume is deleted from the working disk only once two independent copies are proven: the local archive must match the checksum reported for the uploaded object, and the archive must pass its own integrity test. Deletion is never gated on an exit status.

Caveats on the present set

ObservationConsequenceStatus
Support seated too far posterior on patient cases Head depth is measured before the tissue mask is applied, so the couch and head cradle count toward it. The support sits 20–40 mm further back than the nominal 2 mm clearance on the CQ500 cases, and about 80 mm on the GradientHealth ones. Correctable per case by a negative displacement. quantified
Caudal extent limited by the volume The 250 mm axial window is fully occupied by the largest heads, so the most inferior slices of those cases fall at the face of the volume. The window was left unchanged to keep the set dimensionally uniform; the anchored vertex end is unaffected. by design
Age-specific spectra not applied to mesh variants Where the mesh phantoms are exported on the common grid directly, the adult tissue set is used for all ages. Geometry is unaffected; a small number of voxels near the spongiosa–cortical boundary may be assigned differently than an age-specific set would give. by design
